Daughter states after fragmentation

Cluster-tree simulations can continue with a sampled charged daughter:

[default_config.simulation]
simulation_mode = "cluster-tree"
fragmentation_state_method = "phase-space"

The default, fragmentation_state_method = "zero", retains the previous reset of velocity, angular velocity and vibrational energy. The setting is saved with the experiment configuration. It only affects continuing tree transitions; terminal fragmentations and the single-cluster/pathway-at-a-time modes retain their behavior. postfragmentation events record the assigned state using the existing schema.

Model and limits

The sampler extends the differential flux in Eqs. 35–39 of Zapadinsky et al. (2019), Modeling on Fragmentation of Clusters inside a Mass Spectrometer to the simulated parent's instantaneous angular momentum. It treats each molecular product as a spherical rotor, builds vibrational densities of states (DOS) from the existing product frequencies, and assumes an unlimited capture impact parameter, as in Eq. 39. The neutral product is sampled for conservation but is not propagated.

This is an experimental continuation model. Event times and channel probabilities still use the existing rotation-averaged rates, not rates resolved in angular momentum. Consequently the combined model is an approximation, even though each sample conserves energy and both momenta. The paper's agreement of integrated rates with a finite capture model does not validate all daughter-state distributions. The sampled velocities describe separated products, assigned instantaneously at the parent's position and time; molecular separation and torques are not resolved.

Distribution

The mathematical reference gives the full flux derivation. The implemented sampling recipe is summarized here.

For parent velocity \(\mathbf V\), angular momentum \(\mathbf J=I_P\boldsymbol\omega_P\), and internal energy \(E\), the available product energy is \(Q=E-D_0\). The same channel threshold \(D_0\) used by the rate calculation is subtracted once. Parent laboratory kinetic energy is excluded from \(Q\). Product masses must sum to the parent mass, and the sampled daughter's inertia must match its tree payload.

Write \(I_\Sigma=I_D+I_N\), \(I_*=I_DI_N/I_\Sigma\) and \(\mu=m_Dm_N/(m_D+m_N)\). For recoil direction \(\mathbf n\), define

\[ Q_{\mathbf n}=Q-\frac{(\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf n)^2}{2I_\Sigma}, \qquad \rho_{DN}(V)=\int_0^V\rho_D(u)\rho_N(V-u)\,du. \]

The joint density of direction and total vibrational energy is

\[ p(\mathbf n,V)\,d\Omega\,dV\ \propto\ \rho_{DN}(V)(Q_{\mathbf n}-V)^{5/2}\,d\Omega\,dV, \qquad 0\le V<Q_{\mathbf n}. \]

Conditional on these, divide \(W=Q_{\mathbf n}-V\) as \((K,A,R)/W\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,1,3/2)\). Draw \(\mathbf e\) uniformly on the circle perpendicular to \(\mathbf n\) and \(\mathbf a\) uniformly on the sphere. Reconstruct

\[ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol\ell&=\mathbf J-(\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n +\sqrt{2I_\Sigma A}\,\mathbf e,\\ \mathbf s&=\sqrt{2I_*R}\,\mathbf a,\\ \mathbf j_D&=\frac{I_D}{I_\Sigma}(\mathbf J-\boldsymbol\ell)+\mathbf s,\\ \mathbf j_N&=\frac{I_N}{I_\Sigma}(\mathbf J-\boldsymbol\ell)-\mathbf s. \end{aligned} \]

Sample the vibrational split with weight \(\rho_D(u)\rho_N(V-u)\). The daughter receives

\[ \mathbf v_D=\mathbf V+\frac{m_N}{m_D+m_N}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{\mu}}\,\mathbf n, \qquad \boldsymbol\omega_D=\mathbf j_D/I_D, \qquad E_{v,D}=u. \]

These obey \(\mathbf j_D+\mathbf j_N+\boldsymbol\ell=\mathbf J\) and \(K+j_D^2/(2I_D)+j_N^2/(2I_N)+V=Q\), as well as linear momentum conservation. For zero parent angular momentum, integrating out \(K\) at fixed \(\widetilde K=K+A\) gives the paper's kernel \(\widetilde K\sqrt R\,\rho_{DN}(Q-\widetilde K-R)\). The transformed energy \(\widetilde K\) includes rotational energy and must not be used directly as recoil energy.

For an atom-like neutral, its spin and vibrational energy are zero. Omit \(R\) and \(\mathbf s\), use exponent \(1\) instead of \(5/2\), and use \((K,A)/W\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,1)\) with \(I_\Sigma=I_D\).

Numerical implementation and API

The sampler builds ground-inclusive Beyer–Swinehart counts on the existing DOS grid, rounding each oscillator frequency to the nearest bin. Frequencies that round to zero are rejected: reduce bin_width to resolve those modes. Counts at excitation \(i\,\Delta E\) are summed rather than integrated, so the ground state is accessible even with less than one bin of available energy. Individual and combined counts use the same recurrence, ensuring consistent vibrational splits.

The current rate DOS routine omits the all-ground-state contribution; the sampler therefore does not reuse its values. This setting changes neither those tables nor the rates computed from them. Collision sampling also retains its existing bin-centre convention. Refine bin_width when assessing convergence. All runtime energies are joules. Counts and weights are evaluated in logarithms to avoid overflow, including during DOS preparation. Out-of-range energies or missing/invalid product data raise errors; they do not silently fall back to zeroing or clip away energy.

Workflow preparation supplies both products' frequencies and the actual prepared DOS grid, including when loading cached rates. Prepared product data are immutable and shared across realizations. The per-realization random generator controls all sampling, and the rejection loop checks cooperative cancellation.

For direct Python use, construct api.FragmentationProducts(daughter, neutral, bin_width, energy_max) with ClusterData objects and Pint energy quantities (including kelvin), or bare joules. Add products=... to api.MassSpecInputFragmentationPathway, and set fragmentation_state_method=api.FragmentationStateMethod.phase_space on api.MassSpecSubstanceTreeInput. Existing calls retain the zeroing default.
